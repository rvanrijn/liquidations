RSI trendline-break · how it works, and where it actually pays

Dead on the 1-hour.Alive on the 4-hour.

A tidy, mechanical trigger that fires on the RSI. On the 1-hour it's a mirage — nothing survives a real test. Slow the clock to 4 hours and a genuine, out-of-sample-validated edge appears. This is the whole arc: where it broke, where it held, and the one number now deciding its fate.

The mechanism

The whole signal lives on the RSI, not price.

One real example — a bullish break on BTC 1h. The price panel just marks where you'd enter; the bottom panel is where the logic happens.

1

Pivot high

A strict local peak in RSI — higher than the 3 bars on each side. The scanner confirms these as they form.

2

A lower pivot high → the trendline

The next confirmed pivot prints below the first. Two descending peaks define a resistance trendline — drawn through the last 2 pivot highs.

3

The break

On the bar where RSI closes above that line (having been at or below it the bar before), a bullish break fires → LONG.

4

How far RSI punched through the line — the "break strength" column in the signal tables. Here, 4.3 RSI points.

The short side is the exact mirror (below): two ascending pivot lows form a support line; when RSI closes below it → bearish break → SHORT. That's the entire algorithm — Wilder RSI → strict 3-bar pivots → line through the last 2 → fire on the cross. Simple enough to run live; it triggers about 3 times a day.

Does it make money?

Two years, 2,130 signals, every exit rule — mostly red.

Run every signal through a stop-width × exit-rule grid ($5k/trade, 0.02%/side). If the raw trigger had an edge, the grid would be green. It isn't: 10 of 35 cells positive, median −$3.0k. The lone bright corner is the single luckiest knob on a losing surface — worth +0.05% on capital over two years.

The filter that looked like the fix

Add a trend filter and it snaps from red to green.

Take only breaks aligned with the 200-EMA (long above / short below). In-sample on BTC it was startling — 29/35 cells green with the trend, 0/35 against it. Stop-width-independent, corroborated by RSI level. It looked like a genuine, structural edge.

Out of sample, it died

The clean split existed in exactly one window.

The only honest test is data the filter never saw. Re-run it on other BTC windows and other coins. The separation vanishes — and on ETH it inverts.

The "29 vs 0" split was overfit to one BTC regime where trend-alignment happened to coincide with the dominant move. Out of sample that coincidence breaks; on a different asset it reverses outright. The winning side flips every window too — no consistent long or short edge either. Everything so far has been the 1-hour.

The turn · change the clock

On the 4-hour, it's a different animal.

Same scanner, same grid, three timeframes. The red 1-hour surface turns green as you slow it down — slower bars make the pivots significant and the trendlines mean something (the "the line is too twitchy" instinct, fixed by slowing the clock instead of redrawing it). The 4-hour is the sweet spot; the 8-hour is mostly market drift.

This time it holds

Out of sample, across assets, and beating random entry.

The 1-hour "edge" died the moment it saw new data. The 4-hour doesn't. It stays green across independent BTC windows back to 2018 and on ETH — and, the decisive test, it beats a random-entry control (same trade count, same long/short mix, identical exits) in 6 of 7 cases. The gap below is real edge over drift, and it shows up in down markets via the short side — so it isn't just long-beta.

Where the edge lives now

Alive on ETH. BTC is in the longest drawdown of its life.

Eight years of equity, identical rule. The curves track until mid-2025 — then they split: ETH prints a fresh high in April 2026 while BTC rolls over into a 348-day drawdown. Because ETH didn't decay in sync, it isn't a market-wide regime — BTC's weakness is BTC-specific (a regime shift, or alpha decay on the most-crowded asset first). The edge is healthy; it's just migrated.

No longer a backtest

Live now — watching the one number that decides it.

Backtesting is exhausted; the rest is forward proof. A paper trader trades every BTC + ETH 4h break (TP+3% / −2% / 8-day), logs the per-asset split, and recomputes BTC's drawdown against a hard line: its worst-ever drawdown lasted 394 days, then recovered. If BTC crosses 394 without a new equity high, the edge has likely decayed and the strategy stands down on BTC — while ETH keeps running.

348 / 394

The decay line. ~46 days of BTC drawdown left before it exceeds anything the strategy has survived. ETH sits at 66/394 — healthy. The forward tester flags the crossing automatically, so the call gets made by the data, not a hunch.

A real edge — but only on the right timeframe, in the right assets, watched for the day it stops being real.
